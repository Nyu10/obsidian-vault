### 2. Implement the "Lifecycle Investing" Framework

Institutions often use leverage early in a time horizon to maximize expected utility over a multi-decade arc (popularized by Yale economists Barry Nalebuff and Ian Ayres in _Lifecycle Investing_).
- **The Concept:** Young investors are inherently under-diversified across time because their human capital (future earnings) acts like a massive bond portfolio. To balance this, they can afford a modest, steady level of leverage (e.g., 1.2x to 1.3x exposure via low-cost index funds) in their 20s, systematically scaling down the leverage as their portfolio grows and human capital shrinks.  
- **The Execution:** Rather than timing the market, you maintain a fixed, low-level margin buffer (e.g., 15% borrowed against a broad-market index) permanently, letting compound growth outpace the low institutional interest rate.

- **Maximum Allowable Leverage Ratio:** Hard-cap your total debt-to-equity ratio (e.g., never letting total borrowed capital exceed 20% of net liquidation value).
